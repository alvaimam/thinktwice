# ThinkTwice

An AI-powered Terminal coach that helps people practice critical thinking before believing or sharing social-media claims.

## The Unique Kernel
ThinkTwice acts like a theory test before using social media: it gives a learner realistic claim scenarios and trains them to check the reasoning before they accept or share a claim. An LLM creates the scenarios from a JSON guide describing topics and question types, while feedback explains the strongest reasoning and identifies logical fallacies.

## Who It's For
People aged 12 and up who want to use social media and internet services and want practice recognizing suspicious claims and flawed reasoning. The learner uses ThinkTwice directly in Terminal. Parents or government bodies may be future adopters, but they are not the direct users in this proof of concept.

## The Core Loop
The learner starts ThinkTwice in Terminal, receives a realistic social-media scenario generated from the JSON guide, chooses one of four answers, and sees feedback on the best reasoning and the mistakes in the other choices. They repeat this for 35 challenges, then receive a strengths-and-weaknesses summary and a pass/fail result.

## Inspiration & Identity
The driving-license theory test is the reference: practice basic knowledge before taking on a real-world activity. ThinkTwice applies that idea to evaluating claims and reasoning online. The first version is a Terminal experience.

## Why This Matters
The project aims to equip people with critical-thinking and basic-logic skills so they can question suspicious claims before believing or sharing them.

## What "Working" Looks Like
A learner completes a 35-challenge Terminal session. ThinkTwice provides scenario-based questions with four answer choices, explains the reasoning and any logical fallacy in the selected answer, summarizes strengths and weaknesses, and reports pass when the learner reaches at least 80% (28 correct out of 35 if every challenge is scored equally); otherwise it reports fail.

## The POC Boundary
- One learner takes one complete 35-challenge session in Terminal.
- Scenarios are generated by an LLM using project-authored JSON guidance about themes and question types, including misinformation, disinformation, hoaxes, and logical fallacies.
- ThinkTwice uses OpenAI GPT-6 Luna as its default question-generation model. The learner is not asked to select a model during a quiz. Provider switching remains a possible Settings feature, with ThinkTwice choosing a compatible default model automatically.
- Each challenge offers four possible answers and feedback that explains the strongest reasoning and mistakes in the other options.
- The session ends with a strengths-and-weaknesses summary and an 80% pass threshold.
- The POC demonstrates training and assessment; it does not control or grant access to social-media or internet services.

## Later
- Parent or government use as a readiness test before someone begins using social media or the internet.
- A web interface, if wanted after the Terminal proof of concept.

## Explicitly Cut
- Blocking, approving, or managing a person's access to social media or the internet — the POC is a standalone training experience and cannot enforce access rules.
- Parent or government administration — this is a future adoption idea, not needed to prove the learning loop.
- Anthropic and other LLM providers are deferred from the first version.
