# ThinkTwice — Product Requirements

ThinkTwice is a Terminal-based critical-thinking coach and assessment for people aged 12 and up who want to evaluate social-media and internet claims. It turns the approved scope's driving-test analogy into a 35-question practice session. (Source: `scope.md > The Unique Kernel`, `Who It's For`, and `Inspiration & Identity`.)

## The Core Journey

1. The learner starts ThinkTwice in Terminal.
2. ThinkTwice uses OpenAI with the default model `gpt-6-luna`; it does not ask the learner to choose a model. A future Settings flow may allow provider switching while still choosing the provider's default model automatically.
3. Before the session begins, ThinkTwice explains that all challenges are practice scenarios, not real news, and are modeled on situations commonly encountered on social media and the internet. It explains the answer format and that the learner needs at least 80% correct to pass.
4. ThinkTwice presents a short, age-appropriate learning note for each category, then presents 35 LLM-generated questions in seven groups of five. The learner does not choose a category. Each question shows its category, a realistic scenario, and four answer choices labeled A–D. One or more choices may be correct.
5. The learner types one or more choice letters separated by commas, such as `A,B`, then submits the answer.
6. ThinkTwice tells the learner whether the answer is correct and gives feedback on the strongest reasoning and mistakes in the other choices, including relevant logical fallacies.
7. After all 35 questions, ThinkTwice shows the overall score, pass/fail result, and results for each category. For categories with incorrect answers, it explains the missed reasoning so the learner knows what to practice.

The seven categories are: correlation versus causation, statistical reasoning, source credibility, logical fallacies, emotional manipulation, alternative explanations, and AI-generated or manipulated media. (Categories supplied during the PRD interview.)

## Screens and Layout

ThinkTwice has one Terminal interaction surface:

- **Model behavior:** use the configured default model automatically. The learner does not pick a model during the quiz.
- **Before the session:** app name, practice-scenario notice, answer instructions, and the 80% passing requirement.
- **Question view:** question number out of 35, category, scenario, four labeled choices, and a prompt for comma-separated answer letters.
- **Feedback:** correct/incorrect result, explanation of the strongest reasoning, and explanations of mistakes in the other choices.
- **Final summary:** overall correct count and percentage, pass/fail, plus correct/incorrect counts and explanatory feedback for each category.

The output should be readable as plain Terminal text. No visual design direction has been specified.

## Look and Feel

Use clear, respectful language understandable to the stated audience of age 12 and up. Keep questions and explanations focused on helping the learner reason through a scenario. The practice notice must make clear that scenarios are not real news.

## Features and Behavior

### Session and Questions

- A session contains exactly 35 questions, with five assigned to each of the seven categories listed above. (Source: `scope.md > The Core Loop`; category list and distribution supplied during PRD interview.)
- Individual questions and their four answer choices are generated by an LLM from fixed theme and question-type guidance authored in JSON. The question bank is not fixed; the topics/categories are fixed. (Source: `scope.md > The Unique Kernel` and `The POC Boundary`.)
- ThinkTwice uses a configured provider default model automatically; the learner is not prompted to choose a model. The current default is OpenAI `gpt-6-luna`. (Decision made during the build after the provider returned a 400 error for incompatible `gpt-4`.)
- Each question shows its category, scenario, and choices A–D. A question may have one or multiple correct choices.
- The learner enters answer letters separated by commas, for example `A,B`.
- A question is correct only when the learner selects all correct choices. Selecting any incorrect extra choice also makes the response incorrect; questions are all-or-nothing with no partial credit. (Confirmed during the PRD review.)

### Feedback and Results

- After each submitted answer, ThinkTwice reports whether it is correct and explains the strongest reasoning and the mistakes in the alternative choices. It identifies logical fallacies when relevant.
- Every question is associated with one of the seven categories for the final report.
- At the end, ThinkTwice reports the number and percentage correct, and passes the learner at 80% or higher (at least 28 of 35, assuming equal question scoring).
- The final report shows correct and incorrect counts for each category and explains the reasoning behind missed answers. It does not use a separate category-warning cutoff.

## States and Boundaries

- **Provider setup:** if the selected provider key is missing or invalid, explain that the learner must configure a valid provider API key before generation; do not begin the session.
- **Default model unavailable:** explain that the configured default model could not be used and show a sanitized provider detail; do not prompt the learner to choose a different model.
- **Before the first question:** show the practice-only notice, how to enter answers, and the passing threshold.
- **Valid answer:** accept one or more valid choice letters separated by commas, then show feedback and advance to the next question.
- **Invalid answer:** tell the learner the entry is invalid and ask them to check what they typed on the keyboard. Keep the current question open; do not count it or advance.
- **LLM generation error:** show a message that there is currently an LLM error and ask the learner to close Terminal. End the session.
- **Completed session:** show the overall outcome and category-level results.

## Product Decisions

- The app is named **ThinkTwice** and learners interact with it directly in Terminal. (Source: `scope.md > Inspiration & Identity`.)
- ThinkTwice uses its configured default model without asking the learner to choose. The current default is OpenAI `gpt-6-luna`; provider switching can be added later with automatic defaults. Other providers are deferred from the first version.
- The LLM generates individual questions dynamically; JSON guidance defines the themes and question types rather than storing a fixed question bank. (Source: `scope.md > The Unique Kernel`.)
- The session uses 35 questions, five in each of seven categories, and shows the category on each question.
- Learners may select multiple answers by typing comma-separated letters. The assessment requires at least 80% correct to pass.
- Practice scenarios are explicitly identified as not real news before the session.

## What We're Building

- One complete 35-question Terminal session for one learner.
- LLM-generated scenarios and four answer choices guided by the fixed JSON-authored categories and question types.
- Category labels, multi-answer input, immediate feedback, and an end-of-session score, pass/fail result, and category breakdown.
- Clear behavior for invalid input and LLM generation errors.
- A model-selection flow; ThinkTwice uses its configured default automatically.

## Deferred From the POC

- Parent or government administration as a readiness test; the POC is for the learner using ThinkTwice directly. (Source: `scope.md > The POC Boundary` and `Later`.)
- A web interface; the approved first version is a Terminal experience. (Source: `scope.md > Inspiration & Identity` and `Later`.)

## Possible Later Enhancements

Parents or government bodies could use ThinkTwice as a readiness test before someone begins using social media or the internet. A web interface could be explored after the Terminal proof of concept.

## Non-Goals

- Blocking, approving, or managing access to social-media or internet services; the POC trains and assesses but does not enforce access rules. (Source: `scope.md > Explicitly Cut`.)
- Parent or government accounts, dashboards, or administration; those are outside the single-learner proof of concept. (Source: `scope.md > Explicitly Cut`.)
